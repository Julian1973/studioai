## The gates

| Gate | Production stage | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Canon lock | Approved characters, scales, locations, props, costumes and style references |
| 1 | Episode director | Directorial treatment protecting the script's meaning and emotional arc |
| 2 | Scene director | Purpose, emotion, humour, geography, performance and visual progression of every scene |
| 3 | Beat and shot design | Proper animation beats converted into intentional camera shots |
| 4 | Voice and performance | ElevenLabs performances, pauses, reactions, breaths and comic timing |
| 5 | Storyboard and animatic | The entire scene tested for rhythm before expensive images or video are generated |
| 6 | Keyframe production | Reference-accurate opening frame for every shot |
| 7 | Seedance animation | One controlled performance assignment per generated clip |
| 8 | Director and continuity review | Approved takes, timecoded retakes and harvested final frames |
| 9 | Editorial and post | Seamless edit, sound design, music, grading, clean-up and final master |

## 1. The script remains the truth

The director is allowed to elevate the script through: visual humour, reaction shots,
character business, physical comedy, silence and pauses, camera placement, emotional subtext,
environmental storytelling, anticipation/action/payoff.

The system must never casually rewrite dialogue, alter the story's meaning or lose the
emotional payoff.

**Every scene receives a director's statement:**
- What must the audience feel?
- Whose scene is it?
- What changes emotionally?
- Where is the laugh?
- What is the visual surprise?
- What must carry into the next scene?

## 2. Scenes, beats and shots are separate things

- A **scene** is a dramatic unit.
- A **beat** is a change in story, emotion or comedy.
- A **shot** is the camera's view of that beat.

Treating all three as the same thing produced overfilled Seedance prompts and staged,
unnatural performances. **The director first creates the beats. The cinematographer then
decides how those beats should be photographed.**

## 3. Audio and animatic come before final keyframes

The acting performance determines the timing. Voices, reactions, pauses and breaths are
produced first; then a rough animatic tests dialogue timing, comic pauses, shot duration,
emotional rhythm, camera changes, entrances/exits, and scene length.

If a joke does not land in the animatic, a beautiful keyframe will not save it.

## 4. The keyframe is a production asset, not a nice image

Each keyframe must pass a strict visual validation gate: character identity vs references;
height/scale relationships; costume and accessories; location geography; prop positions;
screen direction vs surrounding shots; lighting match; **the pose is the genuine opening pose
of the action**; enough physical space for the intended movement; nothing non-canon.

The opening frame normally shows the **anticipation before the action** — not the payoff.
(Fuzzby begins OUTSIDE the flower, flower positioned for contact — never already buried in
pollen with Seedance expected to reconstruct the gag's physical logic.)

## 5. Seedance receives a self-contained animation contract

Every shot package contains: story purpose, character/reference bindings, opening-frame
truth, emotional performance, physical staging, visibility rules, contact/collision rules,
weight/compression/rebound, camera behaviour, timed progression, dialogue and audio
reference, exact visual payoff, end-of-shot continuity state, prohibited outcomes, clean
editorial handles.

**It receives ONE clear performance assignment — not a mini-film full of camera cuts and
competing actions.**

## 6. Opener and relay logic creates continuity

**Opener shot** — begins from a deliberately generated and approved keyframe. Used when a
scene begins, the location changes, there is an intentional editorial cut, or coverage
changes substantially.

**Relay shot** — begins from a harvested frame of the approved previous animation. Used when
action continues, a mark/object/expression carries forward, geography must stay exact, or a
flowing sequence should feel uninterrupted.

**Every completed shot updates a continuity ledger**: character position, facing direction,
pose, expression/emotional state, costume/accessories, dirt/pollen/water/damage, prop state,
location state, lighting/weather, camera side and eyelines, what enters and leaves frame.

**The @图1 anchor contracts (Julian's dictated wording, 2026-07-16 — LOCKED, verbatim in
`cb_engine.OPENER_ANCHOR` / `RELAY_ANCHOR`, never paraphrased):**

*Opener shot:*
> @图1 is the approved opening keyframe and must be matched exactly as the first frame. Begin
> the planned performance immediately from this pose and composition. Do not redesign, restage
> or introduce unexplained objects or characters.

*Relay shot:*
> @图1 is the approved final frame of the previous shot and must be matched exactly as this
> shot's first frame only. Preserve every character position, scale, pose, expression,
> continuity mark, prop, lighting condition and environmental state.
>
> Immediately after the first frame, continue the story. Do not hold the previous pose
> unnecessarily, replay the previous action, reset the characters or introduce unexplained
> repositioning.

## 7. Cuts are designed, never banned

No arbitrary AI cuts, teleportation or continuity jumps. Every cut or transition is
deliberately designed by the director and editor, motivated through: matched action,
consistent screen direction, correct eyelines, sound bridges, reaction-led cuts, foreground
wipes, camera movement, lighting continuity, clean opening and closing poses.

Trying to force everything into one continuous take is one reason scenes felt forced and
over-staged.

## 8. The director selects performances — not merely usable renders

Each render is reviewed against four separate scores:
- **Canon** — are the characters and world accurate?
- **Direction** — does the acting, humour and emotion land?
- **Physics** — does the action have weight, contact and follow-through?
- **Continuity** — does it connect naturally to the surrounding shots?

A technically clean shot can still fail because the joke does not land. It is rejected or
retaken with a precise timecoded correction — never by rewriting everything.

## 9. Post-production runs across the entire scene

Continuous ambience across cuts, professional SFX and Foley, music shaped to the emotional
arc, dialogue balancing, colour matching, frame clean-up, flicker/artefact repair,
stabilisation where required, transition smoothing, final grade and mix.

**The scene — not the individual clip — is the finished production unit.**

## The system to build

Five intelligent production roles:
1. **Episode Director** — story, theme, emotional arc.
2. **Animation Director** — performance, humour, physical behaviour.
3. **Cinematographer** — shots, lenses, movement, visual progression.
4. **Continuity Supervisor** — every persistent visual and spatial state.
5. **Editor** — rhythm, transitions, sound bridges, final scene flow.

They compile **one structured production package** per scene: director treatment, scene map,
beat map, shot list, performance/audio instructions, keyframe prompts, reference bindings,
Seedance-ready prompts, continuity state, review criteria, retake instructions.

**The next development priority is the Director Engine and the scene-level production
package. Until that layer is right, improving individual Seedance prompts will continue to
give prettier versions of incorrectly directed shots.**

Studio reference architecture Julian supplied, merged with this studio's own locked laws.

- **The deterministic validator** — verbatim dialogue (every locked line assigned exactly
  once, word for word), speaker visibility, dialogue timing, relay-source integrity,
  continuity coverage and drift, unknown characters — zero-LLM, one repair pass max, and
  a package that still fails is written with `validation.passed=false` and must not fire.
- **Kept from this studio, unchanged**: reference-only identity (no appearance text in any
  prompt — rule 5), the platform-length shot contract with the locked @图1 anchor texts,
  ElevenLabs-only voice (@Audio1, no post swap), the render/QA/approval/cost estate.
- **Deliberately NOT adopted from the reference**: its compiled prompt text (dialogue words
  and appearance descriptions in prompts — both violate locked law; its 16-section prompt
  is the wall-of-text shape this pipeline retired).

## Engineering consequences (recorded at lock time, 2026-07-16)

- **The render unit is the SHOT (~4–7s, one performance assignment), not the 15s multi-shot
  beat-take.** The Handle Doctrine's 15s take is retired as the render unit; beats remain the
  story/design unit. This matches Seedance's own documented per-clip guidance (40–110-word
  prompts, ~4–6s per shot) and Julian's proven CapCut workflow.
- **The shipped Seedance prompt returns to platform length.** The 652-word compiled prompt is
  retired. The ACTION content stays ~90–160 words; the fixed @图1 anchor contract (§6, Julian's
  dictated wording, 2026-07-16) adds ~34 (opener) / ~62 (relay) words of continuity scaffolding
  on top, so a full shipped contract runs ~140–210 words (`cb_engine.MAX_SHOT_PROMPT_WORDS =
  210`, a hard assertion). Identity comes from references; staging/continuity come from the
  keyframe and the shot contract's few precise lines — never from walls of defensive text.
- **Negatives are per-shot and few (0–4 beat-specific items + a minimal standing set), not a
  21-item list.**
- **The gate order changes**: voice and animatic (Gates 4–5) now come BEFORE keyframes
  (Gate 6). Timing drives images, not the other way round.
- Existing mechanisms carried forward: dialogue verbatim lock, reference-first identity (no
  character description in prompts), the relay/harvest chain, rejection archiving, the
  one-render economy per shot (with the four-score review deciding keep/retake).

## THE PROBABILISTIC CANDIDATE MODEL (Julian's foundational correction, 2026-07-16)

**Seedance is a probabilistic generator, not a deterministic renderer.** Nothing upstream —
planning, validation, prompt compilation — guarantees the resulting performance; it controls
the INPUTS. The product of this system is therefore NOT a "perfect Seedance prompt": it is
**an approved shot produced through a controlled candidate-generation, evaluation and
selection loop.** Unit tests prove orchestration, validation, spending controls and state
transitions — they are never presented as proof of creative render quality.

**Gate 5 is reclassified: THE TIMING SLATE** (not a creative animatic). It supports approval
of exactly five things — dialogue accuracy, character voice assignment, broad shot duration,
overall scene length, intended dialogue position — and must never be claimed to prove
physical comedy, staging, animation, geography or final rhythm. No large programmatic
storyboard system is built at this stage.

**The candidate generator (cb_render.fire_shot):** one approved shot contract produces a
controlled candidate set — default three, configurable one to four. Before generating, the
SPEND DISCLOSURE is shown (candidate count, cost per candidate, maximum batch cost, prompt
version, references in slot order, opening keyframe/anchor, audio asset, shot duration) and
**explicit human spending approval is required** (--approve-spend / the Studio's "Approve
spend & fire"). Every candidate in a batch uses the identical approved keyframe, references,
audio, prompt and settings — only the model's own variation differs. Provider generation
identifiers are recorded where exposed (.gen.json sidecars); deterministic seed support is
never assumed.

**The prompt stays lean** (reference roles, exact opening state, one primary physical
objective, one comic/emotional beat, one camera intention, audio instruction when relevant,
the lean universal constraints, triggered conditionals only). **Nothing is auto-appended
after a failure** — the former automatic retake-note injection is removed; a reroll uses the
UNCHANGED package — and no permanent negative rule is ever created from a single failed
generation. Shot-specific failure preventions stay capped at three.

**THE FAILURE DECISION LADDER** (cb_render.DECISION_LADDER, printed on every rejection):
one candidate succeeds → approve and harvest it · failures vary randomly → ONE controlled
reroll, unchanged package · identity/opening-geography fails consistently → correct the
keyframe or references · action/physical timing fails consistently → simplify or divide the
shot · the model repeatedly ignores an instruction → remove conflicts, shorten the prompt ·
**after two failed candidate batches → STOP: the shot is MODEL-LIMITED** (a hard status;
fire and the scene walk both refuse) and requires human redesign or an alternative
production method. There is no endless prompt-patching loop.

**Candidate evaluation (§ per candidate, human review — the machine never auto-approves
creative quality):** character identity · relative scale · starting geography · action
readability · physical cause and effect · comic/emotional performance · camera behaviour ·
dialogue and mouth performance · continuity · final-frame usability. Each candidate carries
this checklist as its .review.json, criteria null until a human fills them; machine notes
hold only what is mechanically checkable.

**THE SIX SPEND PROTECTIONS (Julian's non-negotiables, 2026-07-16 — all engine-enforced,
proven by test_golden_path):**
1. **Server-side, single-use, binding-exact approval.** The disclosure step issues a
   server-stored token bound (sha256) to the exact package hash, provider, model/tier,
   candidate count, cost rate, max batch cost, the CONTENT hashes of the anchor/references/
   audio, the slot order, duration and settings. A fabricated token is refused; anything
   changing between disclosure and generation makes the token STALE and refused; the token
   is consumed the moment the batch completes.
2. **Resumable, idempotent batches.** Batch state persists per candidate; a provider failure
   is recorded and the batch stays "generating" — resuming under the ORIGINAL token
   generates only the missing candidates, never regenerating or repaying completed ones.
3. **No automatic approval, ever.** The reviewer selects one candidate or rejects all; only
   a separately selected and approved candidate is harvested for continuity.
4. **Unchanged reroll verified; revision = new version.** The disclosure states
   rerollOfUnchangedPackage from the binding hash; any change re-runs the deterministic
   validator against the CURRENT package content (never a stale green stamp), increments
   packageRevision, and requires fresh approval.
5. **Unconfirmed billing hard-blocks.** An unconfirmed billing profile (planConfirmed/
   cadenceConfirmed) REFUSES all paid generation — voice, keyframe and candidates alike —
   never merely warns. (2026-09-03: byteplus, google, elevenlabs and fal are confirmed in
   engine/billing_profile.json; the sentence that used to say "both providers sit unconfirmed"
   was stale.)
6. **Everything persists.** Every candidate, its review sheet, its disclosure, and every
   provider FAILURE is recorded in the ledger; rejected candidates are archived, never
   deleted; provider request records ride the .gen.json sidecars.

**Whether the pipeline works is MEASURED, not asserted** (cb_render.py metrics <scene>):
candidates generated per approved shot · cost per approved second · time to approval ·
failure categories · prompt revisions · shot redesigns (model-limited count) · character and
continuity failure rates. The architecture is successful only if these improve over real
production.
