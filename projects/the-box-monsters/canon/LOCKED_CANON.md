THE BOX MONSTERS

Series Positioning
Inside Out meets The Borrowers in a hidden cardboard world.
A funny, heartfelt animated series for 8-12s about four handmade monsters who live inside The Fold, a secret cardboard world that opens whenever a child hides a feeling too big to carry alone.

Format
7 minute animated episodes
Digital-first production model
Each episode built from 5 scenes and 14 x 30 second shots
Each scene has one clean plate and one wide character keyframe
Shots within a scene use last-frame-to-first-frame continuity

Core Audience
Primary: 8-12
Sweet spot: 7-10
Co-viewing: parents and carers
Tone: emotionally intelligent, funny, warm, not preschool, not overcomplicated

The Premise
Every child has a box. A shoebox, a moving box, a memory box, a drawer, a lunchbox, a cardboard castle under the bed. When a child hides a feeling they cannot face, the box quietly opens a hidden emotional world.

Deep inside the cardboard city known as The Fold, four Box Monsters feel the call. Patch, Rumble, Tilly and Nib travel through folded cardboard routes into the feeling world inside the box. They never appear directly to the child in the real world. Instead, they work unseen, helping the feeling change shape so the child can carry it.

The Show Mechanic
A child experiences a difficult feeling.
The child hides the feeling in a box.
The box sends a call to The Fold.
The Box Monsters enter the emotional world inside the box.
The feeling becomes visible as a place, creature, object or problem.
The monsters try to fix it, usually in the wrong way first.
They realise what the feeling really needs.
They repair the emotional world.
The child wakes or returns changed, without knowing why.
The magic remains secret.

Core Rule
The child never sees the Box Monsters in reality.
The monsters work inside the hidden emotional world of the box.
The child may appear inside that world only as an emotional echo.

The World

The Fold
The Fold is a secret city made of cardboard, tape, paper, string, labels, old boxes, packing peanuts, pencil stubs, lost buttons and warm little lamps. It exists in the spaces between boxes. It is handmade, practical, funny and alive.

It should feel like a place built by creatures who repair feelings using whatever they can find.

Emotional Worlds
Every box opens into a different emotional world. These worlds are built from the child’s feeling.

Shame might become a cardboard classroom where one wrong word grows enormous.
Fear might become a hallway that gets longer when you look at it.
Jealousy might become a tower where everyone else seems higher up.
Sadness might become a room full of unopened letters.
Anger might become a boiler made of red paper and rattling tape.

The emotional world must always be simple enough for a 7 minute episode.

Main Characters

Patch
The brave first responder. Patch is quick, warm, impulsive and loyal. He jumps in before he has a full plan, then learns by staying with the feeling instead of rushing past it.

Emotional engine: courage and fear.
Comedy: enthusiastic overconfidence, heartfelt mistakes.
Role: leads the team into the box.

Rumble
The biggest monster with the softest heart. Rumble is gentle, careful and protective. He is strong, but his real gift is making space feel safe.

Emotional engine: worth and gentleness.
Comedy: sincere understatement, unexpected tenderness.
Role: protects the child’s emotional space.

Tilly
The organiser and inventor. Tilly loves systems, tools, labels and machines. She often tries to solve feelings like puzzles, then learns that some feelings need listening before fixing.

Emotional engine: control and uncertainty.
Comedy: clever, dry, slightly flustered when plans fail.
Role: diagnoses the box world and builds practical solutions.

Nib
The smallest, fastest and strangest. Nib is curious, funny and easily distracted, but often says the emotional truth by accident.

Emotional engine: stillness and exposure.
Comedy: odd logic, quick remarks, tiny chaos.
Role: breaks tension and finds the sideways truth.

Jenny
Pilot child lead. Jenny is thoughtful, sensitive and quietly funny. In the pilot, she misreads the word “bridge” as “briggle” and feels ashamed. Her journey is learning that getting something wrong does not make her small.

Jenny does not see the monsters.
Inside the shoebox world, she appears only as an emotional echo.

Pilot Episode

Title
The Box That Felt Small

Emotion
Shame

Story
Jenny misreads a word in class and feels embarrassed. She hides the worksheet in her shoebox. That night, The Fold hears the call. The Box Monsters enter the shoebox world and find a cardboard classroom where the word BRIGGLE has grown enormous.

At first, Tilly tries to solve the problem with a Correct Answer Machine, but it only makes Jenny’s shame heavier. Rumble holds the room open. Patch realises Jenny does not need perfection. Nib turns “briggle” into a joke, and Jenny’s emotional echo almost smiles.

The machine becomes a Try Again Machine. A golden card appears: Wrong is not the same as small. In the morning, Jenny finds the repaired worksheet and tries again.

Production Model

Each 7 minute episode is built as:

5 scenes
5 clean plates
5 wide character keyframes
14 shots
30 seconds per shot

Each scene must have:

Scene plate
The location with no characters.
Character keyframe
Same location, wide framing, characters placed for the first shot.
Chained shots
Each shot’s final frame becomes the next shot’s first frame.

Visual Rule
Always wide. Give the shot room to breathe. No cropped heads, hands, feet, ears or key props. The characters need space to perform.

Tone And Humour
The humour should come from character behaviour, not gags forced on top. It should feel warm, dry, slightly wonky and emotionally true.

Not noisy.
Not babyish.
Not sarcastic.
Not over-explained.

The funny sits on the surface. The feeling sits underneath.

Emotional Rule
The monsters never remove the feeling. They help the child understand it, soften it or carry it differently.

The repair is not: “You are fixed.”
The repair is: “This feeling is not bigger than you.”

Series Engine
Every episode follows:

One child
One box
One feeling
One hidden emotional world
One small repair
One secret return to ordinary life

Series Promise
The Box Monsters turns children’s hidden feelings into funny, handmade adventures, helping young audiences understand that even the feelings they hide can be met with care, courage and a little bit of cardboard magic.
