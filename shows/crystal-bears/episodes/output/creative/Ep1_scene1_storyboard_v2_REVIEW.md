# Ep1 · Scene 1 — Storyboard, Process v2
_Generated by the rebuilt creative room (whole-scene-treatment process) from the unchanged script and canon, supplied only the ambition: **“kinetic, exhilarating flower-corridor comedy in which Fuzzby's speed and overconfidence create the physical consequences, while Zenny's economy provides relational contrast”** · state: **awaiting-human-storyboard-approval** · 5 beats · 8 shots · 8 voice performances · escalation: none_

## Gate 1 — the three whole-scene treatments (verbatim)


### 1. The Corridor Can Barely Keep Up

**Audience experience:** A kinetic, exhilarating opening that makes children feel as if they have been swept into Fuzzby’s flight path rather than seated safely outside it. The fun is breathless: the flower corridor is too tall, too springy, too fragrant, and Fuzzby is too certain. Zenny becomes the audience’s handrail — the one calm point that lets the chaos stay readable and fun instead of noisy.
**Emotional point of view:** The scene belongs to Fuzzby’s borrowed importance. He wants routine pollen collecting to prove he is impressive, official and unshakeable. His need, which he cannot admit, is to be enjoyed even when his grand self-image immediately collapses. Zenny’s point of view enters as corrective truth: she is not trying to defeat him, only to name reality with devastating economy.
**Comic/dramatic mechanism:** Primary mechanism: escalating overcommitment. Every time Fuzzby tries to convert a mistake into mastery, he invests harder and makes the visible consequence bigger — ricochet, moustache, smear, branch, blossom, full pollen coverage. Secondary mechanism: movement against stillness. His frantic self-importance keeps orbiting Zenny’s contained judgement. The thunder is the dramatic turn: the world interrupts the game with a sound neither bee controls.
**Character-performance strategy:** Fuzzby’s performance is thought-first but body-led: each boast begins as a sincere decision to recover status. His chest leads before his flight does; his altitude cannot hold a clean line because his confidence is always outrunning his balance. After each impact, he does not absorb failure immediately — he conceals it by re-titling the outcome as success. Zenny spends almost nothing: a look, a measured arrival, one delayed smile she tries not to give away. Her affection is visible only because she withholds it so precisely until Fuzzby earns the crack.
**Visual grammar:** The visual grammar is velocity versus judgement. The frame is allowed to be pulled, banked and momentarily outpaced by Fuzzby, but it regains clarity whenever Zenny becomes the anchor. Flowers are not background; they are vertical architecture that makes Fuzzby’s tiny ambitions feel operatic. No fixed screen lanes: geography is preserved through stems, flower mouths, leaf bends and the repeated relationship of Fuzzby’s erratic path to Zenny’s calmer station, not through permanent left/right assignment.
**Camera–character relationship:** The camera mostly follows and struggles to keep up with Fuzzby, slightly late to his overcommitment so his speed feels bigger than the frame. At comic punctuation points, the camera abandons him for Zenny because her registration is the joke’s final word. When thunder arrives, the camera stops chasing Fuzzby’s self-authored story and lets the sky claim authority for the first time.
**Movement vs stillness:** Fuzzby is never neutral in the air: he overshoots, corrects too hard, ricochets, recovers with false dignity, then launches before the last consequence has fully settled. Zenny’s stillness is active thinking, not inactivity. Her economy makes his motion legible. The scene’s first half is mostly motion punctured by stillness; after thunder, stillness begins to spread into the corridor and Fuzzby’s motion becomes a smaller thing inside a larger warning.
**Depth & environment:** Depth is used as a playground tunnel. Fuzzby repeatedly disappears behind stems, bursts through pollen, gets swallowed by flower mouths and reappears closer or farther than expected. The environment proves weight and consequence: leaves bend, petals clamp, pollen hangs, branches return energy. The incoming storm alters depth by flattening the warm corridor slightly, draining distance into a greyer, more watchful space.
**Rhythm & escalation:** The rhythm starts as a busy chant made physical: quick commitment, impact, proud cover-up. Each cycle gets less controlled and more decorated by consequence. Zenny’s reactions are held just long enough to let children anticipate her verdict before she gives it. The smile is rationed as a release. Thunder breaks the comic rhythm cleanly; after that, Fuzzby tries one more bravado beat, but the scene has already changed scale around him.
**Cut philosophy:** Cuts are motivated by impact, changed emotional ownership, and comic punctuation — never by coverage habit. We cut when Fuzzby’s consequence creates new information, when Zenny’s response becomes the true point, or when the storm alters the scene’s power. Some flight passages may run continuously only when uninterrupted travel is the joke; otherwise, fresh energy enters already in motion.
**Memorable opening image:** Fuzzby already in committed travel down the tall flower corridor, chest leading and body pitched into speed, with the corridor rushing at him as if his confidence has no brakes; Zenny is present as a calm counter-shape in the same world, not as a waiting audience member.
**Memorable closing image:** Fuzzby stuck again in a flower after declaring pressure his specialty, pollen and petals proving the lie, while Zenny’s sigh lands beside him and the dimmer storm-light reaches into the corridor.
**The Cinematographer's challenge:** Do not make this a safe gag parade viewed from outside. If the camera waits politely for each crash, we have flattened Fuzzby into a stunt and Zenny into a reaction insert. The lens must feel implicated in his momentum, then choose Zenny with editorial confidence when her stillness matters. The challenge is to keep the chaos readable for young viewers while refusing conventional action-consequence-reaction coverage.

### 2. Zenny’s Quiet Gets Invaded

**Audience experience:** A comedy of patience under siege. Children laugh because Fuzzby keeps bursting into a calm world that seems to have been doing perfectly well without his ceremony. Adults enjoy the precision: Zenny is not ‘less animated,’ she is thinking faster than Fuzzby while moving less. The scene feels cleaner, drier and more observational than the first treatment, with Fuzzby’s chaos arriving as repeated invasions of her order.
**Emotional point of view:** The governing experience is Zenny’s. She wants to complete the pollen task without feeding Fuzzby’s self-importance. Her need is to allow affection to show without surrendering her truthful register. Fuzzby still wants status, but we experience him largely as the weather system Zenny must process. The thunder matters because Zenny understands it before Fuzzby can turn it into performance material.
**Comic/dramatic mechanism:** Primary mechanism: deadpan observation. Motion meets a still witness, and the still witness is the punchline. Secondary mechanism: concealment to revelation — Fuzzby exits frame with confidence and returns wearing, smearing or dragging the consequence. The dramatic mechanism is anticipation through restraint: Zenny’s tiny changes tell us when play has become warning.
**Character-performance strategy:** Fuzzby performs at full commitment, but we often catch him at the moment his status pitch reaches Zenny and fails. His lines are not delivered to empty air; they are attempts to recruit her into the official version of events. Zenny’s acting is micro-decision work: a blink as calculation, an eye shift as weather assessment, a smile suppressed until it leaks out despite her better judgement. Her storm warning is small because she does not need to inflate truth.
**Visual grammar:** The scene’s grammar is composed stillness disturbed by streaks of self-importance. The camera gives Zenny’s calm enough space to become funny, then lets Fuzzby puncture that calm unpredictably from depth, height or off-screen. The corridor is arranged around Zenny’s work rhythm first; Fuzzby keeps misreading that rhythm as a stage built for him. Readability comes from repeated environmental landmarks, not fixed character sides.
**Camera–character relationship:** The camera stays with Zenny’s patience for longer than a conventional gag would dare. Fuzzby is often discovered by his effects before his full arrival: pollen drifting in, a flower shuddering, a leaf snapping back, his chant approaching at the wrong altitude. When the scene needs Fuzzby’s commitment, the camera gives him enough travel to prove cause; when it needs the laugh, it returns to Zenny’s processed judgement.
**Movement vs stillness:** Zenny’s stillness is the scene’s active force. The longer she holds, the more ridiculous Fuzzby’s orbit becomes. Fuzzby’s movement is not random; every pass is a new attempt to make the corridor acknowledge his rank. Stillness finally changes meaning when thunder arrives: Zenny’s stillness becomes attention, not judgement, and Fuzzby’s motion briefly looks small against what she has heard.
**Depth & environment:** Depth is used for arrival and revelation. Fuzzby can be heard, felt or partially glimpsed before he fully invades Zenny’s composed plane. Flowers in the foreground briefly hide the mistake so the reveal can land cleanly. The environment participates as Zenny’s evidence file: bent leaf, disturbed pollen, trembling branch, blossom swallowing him. The storm creeps in as a change in the upper depth of the corridor rather than an instant palette switch.
**Rhythm & escalation:** The rhythm is slower-burn than the chase version. We hold Zenny long enough for the audience to predict she will not be impressed, then Fuzzby overcomplicates that expectation by becoming even more impressively wrong. The smile is the scene’s first emotional release. Thunder then creates a longer, cleaner silence that children can feel before the final bravado crash restores comedy under a new shadow.
**Cut philosophy:** Cuts are acts of judgement. We cut away from Fuzzby when believing his version any longer would be indulgent; we hold on Zenny when her silence is funnier than explanation. Reveals are protected: the moustache, the smear and the stuck-in-flower outcomes each get their own clean visual idea. No habitual alternating; the scene cuts only for a changed understanding, a reveal, or the storm’s altered power.
**Memorable opening image:** Zenny calmly working within the tall flowers, the corridor breathing with soft pollen and small practical sounds, until Fuzzby’s chant and accelerating disturbance tear into her quiet from somewhere just out of view.
**Memorable closing image:** Zenny’s sigh held in storm-dimmed quiet while Fuzzby’s latest flower-prison rustles beside her — the world has become serious, and he is still trying to be the headline.
**The Cinematographer's challenge:** The danger here is mistaking restraint for safe television stillness. If the camera merely parks on Zenny, the scene dies. Her stillness must be framed as active authorship: the space waits with her, listens with her, judges with her. The challenge is to let Fuzzby be wild without letting him steal the whole point; the funniest image may be the calm frame after he has blasted through it.

### 3. The Flower Corridor Joins the Act, Then the Storm Takes Over

**Audience experience:** A playful living-obstacle-course comedy that gradually turns into a weather warning. Children feel the rainforest corridor as a mischievous partner in the fun: leaves spring, petals clamp, pollen paints, branches answer back. The exhilaration comes less from chasing Fuzzby personally and more from watching the entire environment collaborate with his overconfidence. The final thunder gives the scene its first tummy-drop.
**Emotional point of view:** The emotional point of view is shared between the bees and the corridor itself. Fuzzby believes the world is his proving ground; the world keeps replying, ‘Are you sure?’ Zenny understands the environment better because she does not try to dominate it. Her economy is not only personality contrast — it is ecological intelligence. The storm’s arrival reframes the corridor from playground to early-warning system.
**Comic/dramatic mechanism:** Primary mechanism: environmental participation. Fuzzby’s body causes the world to respond, and the world’s response becomes the joke: ricochet, pollen costume, smear expansion, branch interruption, blossom trap. Secondary mechanism: status change. Fuzzby repeatedly crowns himself competent; the corridor immediately re-titles him. The dramatic mechanism is tonal takeover: thunder converts playful environmental feedback into real environmental warning.
**Character-performance strategy:** Fuzzby treats each flower like an audience and each obstacle like a chance to demonstrate technique. His recovery poses are attempts to overwrite the physical evidence around him. Zenny is tuned to the corridor: her eyes go where the next consequence is forming, and later to the sky before Fuzzby fully registers the sound. She does not fight the environment; she reads it. Her small smile is affection for the whole ridiculous system — Fuzzby plus flowers — not mockery.
**Visual grammar:** The grammar is cause-and-response across a living vertical maze. The camera gives the environment agency: petals can occlude, leaves can redirect, stems can divide the frame, pollen can become a temporary curtain. Fuzzby is not simply moving through space; he is activating it. Zenny’s stillness is placed where the corridor’s reactions can pass around her, confirming that she belongs to the rhythm Fuzzby keeps disrupting.
**Camera–character relationship:** The camera sometimes follows Fuzzby, sometimes leads environmental response, and sometimes arrives late to an aftermath already being completed by the flowers. It does not choose one safe authority. Early on, the camera is curious about what the corridor will do next. As thunder approaches, the camera begins to anticipate the sky and upper leaves, letting the audience sense that the environment knows something before Fuzzby does.
**Movement vs stillness:** Movement is distributed beyond character bodies. Fuzzby launches; leaves recoil; pollen blooms; stems sway; petals close; Zenny barely shifts; then the storm sends a larger movement through everything at once. This contrast lets the final warning feel earned: until now the world’s motion has been comic, but the thunder gives that same responsive world a serious voice.
**Depth & environment:** Depth carries the main story. Foreground petals hide consequences until they pop into readability. Midground stems measure Fuzzby’s wrong turns. Background sky slowly changes pressure and colour. The corridor begins tall and warm, with deep playful layers; after thunder, those layers compress as the storm flattens the distance and makes the flowers feel like they are listening.
**Rhythm & escalation:** The rhythm is musical and cumulative: Fuzzby’s chant, impact, environmental reply, Zenny’s dry punctuation. Each gag adds a new environmental instrument — leaf, pollen, branch, blossom — until the thunder introduces a sound that does not belong to the game. After that, Fuzzby’s ‘pressure’ bravado becomes deliberately smaller and funnier because the audience now knows the corridor is no longer only playing.
**Cut philosophy:** Cuts are motivated by environmental handoffs. A bending leaf earns the next image, a pollen burst earns the reveal, a branch’s recoil earns the tumble, a skyward tremor earns the storm turn. We avoid mechanical character coverage; the scene cuts when the world has new evidence to present or when emotional authority passes from Fuzzby’s self-story to Zenny’s weather-reading.
**Memorable opening image:** The flower corridor alive before any explanation: pollen drifting, leaves held under tension, blossoms stacked like soft towers — then Fuzzby’s committed flight enters and immediately starts setting the whole place off.
**Memorable closing image:** A blossom holding Fuzzby in the foreground as the surrounding flowers tremble under the first storm-dark breath; Zenny’s sigh is small, but the corridor’s hush makes it feel shared by the whole place.
**The Cinematographer's challenge:** Do not reduce the environment to decoration behind bee business. If leaves, petals and pollen merely ‘react’ after the fact, we have missed the scene’s most original engine. The challenge is to photograph the corridor as a comic partner with timing, weight and memory, while still keeping Fuzzby’s intention and Zenny’s judgement unmistakable for the youngest viewer.

## Gate 2 — the Showrunner's selection

**Selected:** Combined treatment: “The Corridor Can Barely Keep Up” governed by “The Flower Corridor Joins the Act”
**Combined from:** 1. The Corridor Can Barely Keep Up, 3. The Flower Corridor Joins the Act, Then the Storm Takes Over
**Governing audience experience:** Children feel swept inside Fuzzby’s overconfident flight path through a living flower corridor that keeps answering back, with Zenny as the calm handrail, until thunder makes the same playful world suddenly listen.
**Rationale:** Treatment 1 gives the scene the right opening engine: Fuzzby’s speed, borrowed importance, and self-rebranding failures drive the comedy from character rather than stunt work. It best matches the episode’s false-bravado setup and gives Zenny the needed relational contrast: she is not a reaction machine, she is the clean truth that makes his performance lovable. Treatment 3 should be folded in because it gives the scene its freshest visual identity: flowers, leaves, pollen, stems and blossoms are not scenery but comic participants with weight, timing and memory. The winning version is Fuzzby as the cause, the corridor as the consequence-maker, and Zenny as the reader of truth. This preserves the kinetic ambition while making the world feel specific to Crystal Cove, and it earns the thunder turn because the environment has already been speaking before it becomes warning. Treatment 2 has strong Zenny precision, but as the governing approach it makes the scene too observational and risks cooling the opening when the episode needs an exhilarating launch. Its best lesson is restraint: Zenny’s stillness must remain active, rationed, and affectionate.
**Rejection checks:** Rejected as sole treatments: Treatment 2, because its “quiet invaded” frame risks making Fuzzby a recurring disruption to Zenny rather than the scene’s engine; it can drift toward parked-camera patience and Fuzzby-action/Zenny-judgement repetition if over-literalized. Treatment 3 alone, because sharing emotional point of view with the corridor risks turning the scene into a clever obstacle course and thinning the Fuzzby/Zenny relationship heart. Treatment 1 alone is not enough unless the corridor gains the agency and tonal takeover described in Treatment 3. The selected combined approach rejects fixed character lanes, keeping all characters visible, habitual action/consequence/reaction coverage, safe camera placement, repeated close-up punctuation, mechanical shot counts, and generic “cinematic” illustration. The camera must discover momentum and consequence experientially; cuts happen only when ownership, evidence, or scale changes.

## Gate 3 — beat architecture (inside the selected treatment)


### 1.B1

**Source script:** Fuzzby and Zenny collect pollen in the tall rainforest flower corridor; Fuzzby turns a routine dip into a leaf ricochet and recovers as if the crash proves mastery, while Zenny arrives to frame him with one flat look.
**Dialogue (verbatim-locked):** “FUZZBY: BIZZY-BIZZY-BIZZY, BIZZY-BIZZY-BIZZY…” · “FUZZBY: Nailed it.”
**What changes:** A normal pollen-collecting run becomes Fuzzby’s self-declared demonstration of importance. The corridor answers his confidence physically: the leaf takes his weight, throws him off-line, and leaves him needing to re-title a crash as success.
**Who drives:** Fuzzby drives the change through overcommitted confidence; the leaf supplies the visible contradiction, and Zenny completes the beat by making stillness the verdict.
**Audience anticipation:** Children anticipate that his busy chant means mastery is coming, then feel the fun of the corridor being too springy and too alive for his certainty. They wait for whether he will admit the ricochet or sell it as planned.
**Action / choice:** Fuzzby charges into the flower corridor already pitched forward, chest leading and altitude bobbing too hard to be controlled. He dips for pollen with parade-ground importance, rebounds off a leaf, scrambles his balance in mid-air, then chooses proud concealment instead of embarrassment.
**Consequence:** The corridor has proved his body wrong: leaf bent, flight path knocked crooked, pollen disturbed. Fuzzby lands in a too-proud recovery image, insisting the accident was mastery. Zenny’s arrival with one flat look gives the audience permission to laugh at the cover-up, not the fall.
**Emotional/comic handover:** Comic mechanism: escalating overcommitment with movement against stillness. The handover is Fuzzby inheriting a fragile proud pose that now needs defending, while Zenny’s dry presence promises the next puncture.

### 1.B2

**Source script:** Fuzzby rockets from his last proud pose into another flower and pulls out wearing a readable yellow pollen handlebar moustache and goatee; Zenny’s dry answer cracks the formality he is trying to project.
**Dialogue (verbatim-locked):** “FUZZBY: Do I look official?” · “ZENNY: Yes Fuzzby Officially nuts!”
**What changes:** Fuzzby’s attempt to look official becomes visually ridiculous: the corridor gives him a pollen moustache and goatee that make his borrowed importance impossible to take at face value.
**Who drives:** Fuzzby drives by launching before his previous recovery has settled; the flower drives the reveal by marking him; Zenny drives the final status change with her dry label.
**Audience anticipation:** The audience expects another impressive rebound because Fuzzby launches as if reset. The anticipation turns when he emerges wearing the consequence, and children wait for Zenny to name what everyone can see.
**Action / choice:** Fuzzby converts the proud pose into another rocket launch, aiming to preserve rank through speed. He punches into a flower mouth and, after the concealment pause, pulls free as though presenting himself for inspection, asking for official confirmation instead of checking the damage.
**Consequence:** The pollen forms a clear handlebar moustache and goatee. His formality collapses under the image before Zenny speaks; her answer re-titles him from self-appointed official to “Officially nuts.”
**Emotional/comic handover:** Comic mechanism: concealment to revelation plus status change. The handover is Fuzzby stung by the verdict but not defeated, carrying a visible pollen costume into the next attempt to recover dignity.

### 1.B3

**Source script:** Fuzzby reacts to Zenny’s verdict by wiping the pollen moustache worse, then barrels into a branch and tumbles upside down into a blossom, popping back up with his self-branded crash line.
**Dialogue (verbatim-locked):** “FUZZBY: Buzz Crash!!”
**What changes:** The small embarrassment becomes a bigger, messier signature. Fuzzby’s effort to erase the evidence spreads it, then the corridor compounds the failure with branch recoil and blossom capture.
**Who drives:** Fuzzby drives the escalation by trying to correct the image without conceding it. The branch and blossom participate as consequence-makers, each returning his force back at him.
**Audience anticipation:** Children anticipate that wiping will fix the moustache, then see immediately that touching it is the worst possible idea. The branch creates a second anticipation: how far can this one correction go wrong?
**Action / choice:** Fuzzby chooses frantic concealment. He scrubs at the pollen with too much force, smearing the moustache wider, then accelerates away before recovering balance. His overcorrection carries him into a branch; the branch bends under him and returns the energy, tumbling him upside down into a blossom.
**Consequence:** The pollen is worse, his orientation is lost, and the blossom briefly owns him. When he pops back up, he brands the whole disaster as his own catchphrase rather than admitting defeat.
**Emotional/comic handover:** Comic mechanism: escalating overcommitment and hiding failure. The handover is louder comic ownership — Fuzzby has turned accident into identity — which sets up Zenny’s restrained smile as the next release.

### 1.B4

**Source script:** Zenny’s eye roll finally cracks into a smile as Fuzzby dives again and emerges even more pollen-covered; a distant thunder rumble interrupts the game, shifting the light and turning their attention toward the unseen storm.
**Dialogue (verbatim-locked):** “FUZZBY: …okay that sounded dramatic.” · “ZENNY: A Storm’s coming.”
**What changes:** The scene turns from comic corridor game to weather warning. Zenny’s withheld amusement releases for a moment, then thunder takes authority away from Fuzzby’s performance and gives it to the sky.
**Who drives:** Zenny drives the first change by finally allowing the smile to crack; the thunder drives the true turn by interrupting both bees and altering the corridor’s scale.
**Audience anticipation:** Children anticipate Zenny’s smile because she has been rationing it; they get that release just before the rumble makes everyone listen. The anticipation shifts from “what will Fuzzby hit next?” to “what is coming?”
**Action / choice:** Fuzzby dives again, still trying to prove the act can keep growing, and emerges even more pollen-covered. Zenny’s eye roll softens into a small, reluctant smile. Then the thunder arrives; Fuzzby’s hover goes crooked first, and he tries to annex the sound into his performance, while Zenny looks toward the sky before moving her body.
**Consequence:** Warm comic motion is interrupted by a greyer, more watchful corridor. Pollen hangs instead of simply sparkling; stems feel taller and stiller. Fuzzby’s joke shrinks against the weather, and Zenny’s plain warning becomes the scene’s new truth.
**Emotional/comic handover:** Comic mechanism into dramatic turn: restraint to release, then environmental interruption. The handover is scale change — the audience carries the laugh of Zenny’s smile into the first small brace of storm concern.

### 1.B5

**Source script:** Fuzzby answers Zenny’s storm warning with pure bravado, then immediately flies into a flower and gets stuck again while Zenny sighs.
**Dialogue (verbatim-locked):** “FUZZBY: Good thing I work well under pressure.”
**What changes:** Fuzzby tries to restore the old comic contract after the storm has changed the scene, but the flower corridor instantly exposes the bravado as false. The warning now remains underneath the joke.
**Who drives:** Fuzzby drives the attempted reset with pure self-importance; the flower drives the contradiction by catching him; Zenny seals the beat with a sigh rather than another verbal punchline.
**Audience anticipation:** The audience knows the boast cannot survive contact with the corridor, especially now that the light has shifted. They anticipate the immediate proof that pressure is exactly what Fuzzby does not work well under.
**Action / choice:** Fuzzby puffs his chest into a brave, official little stance and launches as if pressure is his natural element. He over-accelerates straight into a flower before the boast has emotionally cleared the air, and the petals clamp or hold him in place.
**Consequence:** He is stuck again, pollen and petals proving the lie in the same image. Zenny’s sigh lands beside him as affectionate truth: she does not need to correct him because the corridor has done it for her.
**Emotional/comic handover:** Comic mechanism: anticipation to reversal with environmental participation. The handover leaves Fuzzby visibly trapped inside his own bravado while storm-light reaches into the corridor, carrying the episode out of pure play and toward the coming danger.

### S1.SH1 — CONTINUOUS · NEW KEYFRAME · beats 1.B1

**Purpose:** Throw the audience into Fuzzby’s self-important pollen run and let the corridor immediately contradict him.
**Audience experience:** Children feel pulled down the flower corridor with him, then get the first laugh when the living world proves faster than his certainty.
**Opening image:** Fuzzby already committed in flight, chest leading into the tall flower corridor as stems rush past; Zenny is a calm counter-shape in the same space.
**Principal performance:** FUZZBY: BIZZY-BIZZY-BIZZY, BIZZY-BIZZY-BIZZY… He treats the dip for pollen like official business, rebounds off the leaf, scrambles his balance, then presents the recovery as triumph: FUZZBY: Nailed it. Zenny arrives with one flat look.
**Camera relationship:** The camera follows and struggles to keep up, losing perfect control during the ricochet, then regains clarity when Zenny becomes the handrail.
**Physical/emotional change:** Routine collecting becomes a performance of borrowed importance; the leaf changes that performance into a cover-up.
**Closing image:** Fuzzby held in a too-proud recovery, leaf still settling behind him, Zenny’s still look making the verdict unavoidable.
**Transition justification:** The launch into the next mistake should keep flowing from this fragile proud pose; cutting here would grant Fuzzby a clean reset, when the joke is that he rockets onward before dignity has actually returned.
**Physical performance (Gate 5):** Fuzzby’s chest arrives a fraction before the rest of him, as if importance is towing the body. He commits to the pollen dip without checking the leaf’s spring; contact compresses him into it, the leaf bows under his weight, then throws him back on a slightly wrong angle. His wings scramble to correct what his pride refuses to acknowledge: he over-levels, dips, catches himself, then locks the chest up and chin forward into a too-official hover while the leaf continues wobbling behind him. Hold “Nailed it” just long enough for the audience to see the leaf still settling and the lie still vibrating through him.
