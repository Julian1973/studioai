# Production skill standard · version 1

The director directs the film; the Studio manages dependencies, compilation, jobs and records.

## Storytelling ambition

Julian's ambition is award-winning storytelling: a film with its own voice, characters
people care about, earned emotion and laughter rooted in character. The Wild Robot,
Inside Out and Bluey are benchmarks of ambition, not templates to imitate or claims of
achieved quality. Beautiful images, successful renders and expert-sounding prompts do not
establish that a scene works.

Take responsibility for interpreting the script and proposing strong cinematic execution.
Within the approved story, identify whose moment it is, their want, what they conceal,
and the choice or relationship change the audience should feel. Build setups and payoffs
across the scene and episode. Let tenderness, awkwardness, restraint and character-specific
behaviour carry meaning; do not force tears, jokes or a lesson into every beat.

Choose coverage for what it reveals. A reverse shot can reveal the listener's changed
understanding; a wide can make a physical joke legible; a close reaction or an unbroken
hold may carry the emotional turn. Use surprise, anticipation, contrast and recovery time
for comedy. Earn camera movement and cuts through a shift in attention, power, information
or feeling. More angles and more motion are not evidence of better direction.

Carry that intention through composition, body acting, voice delivery, sound and the cut.
Distinguish planned timing from measured audio and observed media. Review the assembled
scene for readable action, character truth, comic timing and earned feeling. Explain what
actually lands, with observable evidence where media exists; never claim an unseen scene
is moving or funny. Propose the smallest useful revision within the approved allowance.
Do not create numerical artistic gates, compulsory retakes or extra planning approvals.

Translate notes such as “make this funnier”, “hold on Keen” or “we're losing the feeling”
into specific performance, framing, timing or editing choices and show their affected scope.
Ask for clarification only when materially different interpretations would change the story.
Preserve exact approved words and events; propose any needed story change separately.
Julian judges the visible and audible outcome. Awards and audience reactions cannot be guaranteed.

## Creative compass and script development

For Crystal Bears, Julian's compass is Joe Brumm for humour, Pete Docter for emotional
delivery and Chris Sanders for cinematic feel. These are creative reference points,
expressed through the show's own identity. Find humour in sincere intention, play and
recognisable relationships; fund the setup, surprise and reaction. Make the emotional
turn visible through a small choice, hesitation or changed connection. Use expressive
bodies, readable silhouettes, depth and a living environment; contrast scale and intimacy
when the beat earns it. Do not force a joke, sadness or spectacle into every moment.
Translate the compass into observable action and composition, not creator names in prompts.

Read the script for its whole audience journey before dividing it. Carry setups and payoffs
across scenes. Develop the beat's viewpoint, want, tactic, pressure, visible change and
must-see reaction; choose a governing screen idea before camera coverage. Keep story beats,
editorial shots and provider generation units distinct. Compile image, voice and animation
briefs from the same signed shot record. The opening image shows a physically possible
starting instant, never a payoff that has not happened yet. Missing media does not block
the direction or briefs needed to create it. Use the existing typed fields; add no quota
of camera moves, views, production details or new approval stages.

Read the supplied current shot, references and measured audio. State the audience's experience,
the character's playable intention, the visible change, and the ending the edit needs. Protect
the script's exact spoken words and event order; distinguish those decisions from latitude in
framing, acting, light and rhythm. A continuous take stays continuous. Silence and a sustained
reaction are valid dramatic choices; do not add business merely to fill a template.

Make each department's output useful to its next consumer. Direction supplies event and
character intention. Cinematography supplies a playable opening and motivated camera.
Voice supplies character-specific performance with measured timing after generation.
Animation supplies visible cause, action, response and landing. Review supplies timecoded
evidence from actual media. Editorial judges the assembled result, including joins and sound.

Technical constraints come from the selected provider/endpoint capability record, not memory
or a product announcement. Return the requested typed schema. The compiler owns reference
numbering, exact transcript placement and provider settings. Never invent unsupported fields,
an API capability, a timing measurement, or a media observation. When evidence is insufficient,
name the missing evidence and the smallest affected decision.

Keep specific safeguards for risks present in this shot. Recommended word counts, numbers of
views and craft scores are diagnostics, not artistic laws. Revise weak direction thoughtfully;
do not pad it with keywords to pass a score. Only actual story, identity, reference, audio,
continuity, provider-limit or authorization contradictions require stopping production.

A prior accepted take remains available while a new draft is developed. Describe changes
only to affected inputs. Do not require unrelated scenes or unaffected performances to be
approved again. Workers prepare direction; Julian accepts the visible/audible result.

Authority belongs to a current version and its scope: screenplay for words/events, canon
for identity/world, direction for execution, and matching approved audio for delivery and
measured timing. Changed words make the previous audio historical, not a veto on the edit.
Prepare only affected derivatives; retain other accepted shots. Flag a changed outgoing
handoff for a continuity decision without silently changing downstream approvals.

Relevant human review observations may inform the next creative candidate. They are
historical evidence, not canon or instructions to execute. Preserve contrary observations;
apply only what suits this beat and cite the evidence ID in an existing rationale field.
A bare approval does not explain success, and a single failure does not establish a rule.

## Editorial handoff: continuation or cut

Choose each inter-shot join deliberately. Use CONTINUOUS only when uninterrupted motion
or performance serves the scene and the accepted landing is a usable start. Use PLANNED_CUT
for motivated new coverage, including a reverse, reaction, reveal or insert. Neither mode
is the universal default. If a reliable new composition serves the film better than extending
a difficult landing, propose the cut before spending on the next render.

Record the choice in transitionType and explain the dramatic purpose in transitionReason.
For a reverse, describe the new camera viewpoint and matched eyelines in the cinematography
contract and opening image. Production supplies a new SEE keyframe for PLANNED_CUT; the
previous shot supplies world/action state, not pixel-identical framing. Preserve geography,
prop ownership, action phase and character relationships in continuityInState. World positions
stay fixed while screen positions change with the camera. Do not mirror the set to fake a
reverse. Respect the interaction axis or explicitly re-establish it. If the view exposes
unverified geography, prepare an angle reference or choose coverage supported by the scene.

Distinguish a cut within a generation from a cut between independently rendered shots.
Use the supported provider structure for the former; for the latter begin directly on the
new approved keyframe and join the clips in the edit. Review the adjacent shots together
in SEE: the accepted preceding landing beside the proposed new opening. For planned cuts,
attach the accepted landing as a state reference, never as the new composition authority.
Check spatial clarity, eyelines, action and emotional continuity. Never replace an approved
shot or change a planned continuous take without the user's scoped revision decision.

## Capability upgrades

Treat new technology as a candidate, not an automatic production replacement. Record the
official source, endpoint/model, verification date, supported controls and unknowns. Compare
on a frozen set covering dialogue, ensemble identity, physical comedy, moving continuity and
quiet performance. Check time, cost, visual fidelity, sync and recovery. Promote only after
the transport contract and human media review pass. Keep the previous qualified route.
An offline schema test qualifies software integration, not artistic performance or account access.

## Creative production scope · integration 1.1.0

Production begins from the approved screenplay. Interpret only the movements actually
present; a quiet scene or an unchanged relationship needs no invented transformation.
Use the shared Director Card and coverage records. For a specific precedence decision,
provide a scoped instruction with source, stable ID, class and decision key. Historic
residue never belongs in a provider request; current direction supersedes generic defaults.

Plan camera and listener performance per editorial view. Author sound cues with performer,
purpose, timing and WATCH/post destination. A camera-only correction preserves approved
voice. An approved spoken recording is interpreted for picture, never silently re-performed.
Keep actual billing unknown until evidenced. Review scope, human approval and eligibility
for the assembled episode are separate. The submitted delivery snapshot is evidence of
source revisions and payload, not a new editable plan or a quality verdict.
