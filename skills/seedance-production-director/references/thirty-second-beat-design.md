# Thirty-second beat design

Thirty seconds is a ceiling and a production canvas, not an instruction to crowd the unit. The audio waveform, dramatic turn and physical complexity determine the useful shot count.

## Budget before coverage

Create a timing ledger before writing shots:

```text
Total unit: 30.0s
Opening acquisition: [time]
Spoken segments from @Audio1: [actual in/out ranges]
Silent anticipation/action/reaction: [time]
Transitions and camera redirects: [time]
Landing thought and living hold: [time]
Unfunded time: [must equal zero before delivery]
```

Silence can hold gaze, breath, physical preparation, consequence, recognition and recovery. Do not prescribe a long hold after every line.

## Breathing-space diagnostics

The unit is overcrowded when:

- reaction begins before its audible or visible cause;
- contact, consequence and recovery collapse into one unreadable motion;
- a shot lacks time to establish its subject and purpose;
- the final line ends at or near the final frame;
- multiple emotional turns compete inside one shot;
- the landing hold introduces new action because the payoff did not finish earlier.

Split the beat or simplify coverage instead of accelerating performance unnaturally.

## Shot count

Use one to four motivated shots in a 30-second unit when they deliver one dramatic beat. Four shots are appropriate when each has funded time and a distinct job, for example:

1. establish pursuit or relationship contrast;
2. make physical cause readable;
3. track consequence without interruption;
4. land recovery, reaction and final thought.

One continuous shot may be stronger when continuity, performance or lip-sync matters more than coverage. A cut must reveal consequence, change point of view, clarify geography, land comedy or catch a meaningful reaction.

## Comedy and action

Fund the causal chain separately:

`anticipation → contact → stored force → release → consequence → recovery → self-check → reaction → landing thought`

The audience must see the cause before the payoff. Protect the recognition beat after a comic event. A silent witness may carry the joke through stillness while the active character recovers.

## Landing state

Describe the final 1–3 seconds as a living composition only when actual timing supports it. Record pose, balance, gaze, mouth state, positions, accumulated evidence, camera axis, ambience, final audio state and the next-unit anchor. The landing completes the current beat without beginning the next.
